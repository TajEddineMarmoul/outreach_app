# Outreach homepage, revision 3

A homepage concept aimed at helping visitors understand the product, see a believable example, and feel confident getting started. This is a design hypothesis, not a measured claim of increased signups or sales. Ease of implementation is not the design constraint.

[Open full-page concept](index.html)

## The page story

1. **Hero, unchanged:** establish the promise of personal email from one draft.
2. **Personalization, revised:** show a realistic email for one person, explain where the details come from, and give visitors a clear Get started action.
3. **Scheduling and control, revised:** show planned sending, then answer the concern about losing control of email sending.
4. **FAQ and final invitation, unchanged:** answer remaining questions and offer the next step.

## What changed

The personalization headline is tighter, with two lines instead of three. Repetitive supporting blocks are removed. The body directly explains how the list supplies individual details. Get started appears immediately below the benefit, followed by preview reassurance. Reusable messages remain as a quiet supporting point.

The fictional portraits remain, and Sam's name and company match the email. The calendar moves to the left to vary the rhythm as the visitor scrolls. Sending after launch is explicit. The control strip now leads with Nothing sends until you say so.

The approved palette, sans-serif type, paper objects and cobalt connector remain the design system. There are no extra logos, testimonials, statistics, pricing or performance promises.

## UX intent and limits

- The same Get started action should reach the same signup destination from the hero, personalization section, and closing invitation.
- If implemented, selecting a profile should change the example name and company together, without moving the surrounding page or sending email. Profile portraits are illustrative; they do not promise that the app imports photos.
- Keep preview changes keyboard accessible, clearly indicate selection, and respect reduced motion.
- Recreate the text and controls as accessible HTML and adapt the layout for narrow screens; the PNGs are desktop art direction, not production page assets.
- Keep the note about fictional profiles. Do not use these generated portraits as social proof.
- Scheduling requires launch. Pausing affects future sends, not previously sent email.
- The review page is deliberately static and clearly labeled. Its section navigation and image links work, but artwork buttons are not signup controls.
- No application code, hero, FAQ, authentication, database, deployment, or email-sending settings were changed.
